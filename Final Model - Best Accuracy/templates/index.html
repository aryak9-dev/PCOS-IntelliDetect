About PCOS

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most common hormonal disorders, affecting nearly 1 in 10 women of reproductive age.
It can cause a range of symptoms, including irregular menstrual cycles, hormonal imbalances, and metabolic complications.
Our goal is to help you understand PCOS better and provide tools for early detection and management.

What is PCOS?

PCOS occurs when the ovaries produce excessive androgens (male hormones), leading to multiple small cysts on the ovaries.
This imbalance affects ovulation, leading to irregular periods and other symptoms.
It is a multifaceted condition influenced by genetics, lifestyle, and environmental factors.

Common Symptoms

  • Irregular or missed periods
  • Excessive facial or body hair (hirsutism)
  • Severe acne or oily skin
  • Unexplained weight gain or difficulty losing weight
  • Thinning hair or hair loss on the scalp
  • Fatigue and mood swings
  • Difficulty conceiving or infertility
  • Dark patches of skin on the neck or underarms

Why Should You Care?

PCOS is more than just a reproductive health issue—it can significantly impact your overall well-being.
If untreated, PCOS can lead to:

  • Type 2 Diabetes
  • High blood pressure and cholesterol
  • Heart disease
  • Endometrial cancer
  • Mental health challenges, such as anxiety and depression

FAQs About PCOS

What Causes PCOS?

The exact cause of PCOS is unknown, but it is believed to be linked to genetic and environmental factors.
Insulin resistance, inflammation, and hormone imbalances also play a role.

Can PCOS Be Cured?

PCOS cannot be cured, but its symptoms can be managed effectively through lifestyle changes, medication, and regular monitoring.
Early detection is key to better outcomes.

Is PCOS Only About Irregular Periods?

No, PCOS affects much more than menstrual cycles.
It can influence fertility, skin, weight, and even long-term metabolic and cardiovascular health.

Who Is at Risk?

Any woman of reproductive age can develop PCOS.
However, you may be at higher risk if you have a family history of PCOS, obesity, or a sedentary lifestyle.

How Can I Manage PCOS?

Management typically includes a combination of lifestyle changes (healthy diet, regular exercise),
medications (for regulating hormones or improving insulin sensitivity), and monitoring by healthcare professionals.
